      How this tracker works

      A competing-risk model follows service in office, House impeachment, and departure. Firing and resignation can happen before or after impeachment; Senate removal can happen only after impeachment while still serving. The model keeps those paths separate so departure probabilities cannot be double counted.

      Headlines are collected with the regular forecast pipeline. Evidence reviews can revise the four daily transition rates, with a source and reason for each change, within hard bounds and by at most a factor of two per review. Unchanged evidence does not trigger another review; reviews are spaced at least six hours apart. If a collection or review fails, the last assumptions remain in force and their original review date stays visible.

      Actual impeachment and departure require a separately verified status update; headlines never automatically mark an event as completed. This tracker covers the current tenure before January 20, 2029, excludes impeachment after departure, and treats requested resignations as resignations. Other causes of departure and a return to office are outside the model.

      Long-range estimates depend heavily on the background assumptions. Sensitivity ranges vary each reviewed rate independently between half and twice its value, and vary background rates together by the same factors. They measure assumption sensitivity and have no claimed statistical coverage. The model has not been backtested.

      Inspect the rates and calculation

      For each period, state probabilities are propagated by the matrix exponential exp(Q × days), where Q holds the daily transition rates. The starting rates and background rates are modeling judgments, not measured frequencies. Prediction-market prices and the presidential election model are not inputs.
