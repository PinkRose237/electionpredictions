How this early model works

  • Generic candidates. Both parties' nominees are unknown, so each state is modelled as Generic Democrat vs. Generic Republican. Named-candidate polls are listed on state pages but don't move the average until nominees exist.
  • Fundamentals first. Each state's outlook starts from its Cook PVI (how it voted in 2020/2024 vs. the nation) plus a neutral national environment. State polls and expert ratings blend in automatically once published.
  • Wide early uncertainty. With the election far out, the simulation allows large national swings — the bands are honest about how much can change.
  • Electoral College math. 20,000 correlated simulations decide each state, then count 538 electoral votes; 270 wins. A 269–269 tie goes to the House and counts for neither party.
  • Markets are context only, not a model input — same as the midterm dashboard.

Data sources

  • State lean: Cook Partisan Voting Index (2020 + 2024 results)
  • Polls and expert ratings: Wikipedia, as published (sparse this early)
  • Prediction markets: Polymarket, PredictIt, Kalshi (shown for comparison, not a model input)
  • News: Google News
