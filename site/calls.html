Race Calls

Who has called what. Every major news organization projects winners on its own schedule, and they don't always agree or move at the same speed. This page lines up the calls from four of them for every Senate, House and governor race.

About these calls

  • A call is a news organization's projection of the winner, made by its decision desk from vote counts, its own models and surveys. It is not an official result; states certify results weeks later.
  • A race is marked Confirmed once at least 2 of the 4 outlets have called it for the same candidate and no other candidate has as many calls (a 2–2 split stays unconfirmed). If another outlet has called it differently, that is flagged next to the confirmation. The rest of the site and the Apple TV board count a race as called only once it's confirmed.
  • CNN and NBC News each run their own decision desk. Bloomberg publishes the Associated Press's calls, and the AP column shows the AP's calls as carried by The New York Times. So those two columns usually match, and they can still differ for a few minutes while one site catches up.
  • Lean labels are the AllSides Media Bias Ratings for each outlet's online news coverage, as of November 3, 2026. They rate an outlet's coverage overall, not its decision desk.
  • A winner is shown on the Democratic or Republican side as this site lists the race, so an independent running in place of a party (Nebraska's Dan Osborn) counts on that side.
  • Calls are collected from each outlet's public results page about every 10 minutes and this page checks for new ones every minute. Some calls may appear on an outlet's own site a few minutes earlier.

Data sources

  • Race calls: CNN, NBC News, Bloomberg (AP), and the AP via The New York Times
  • Outlet lean ratings: AllSides Media Bias Ratings
